# PART 1 — LEARN

## Lesson 0: Introduction to DISC

**What DISC is.** DISC is a simple map of how people prefer to interact. It sorts behavior into four patterns: D, I, S, and C. It is not about labeling people or putting them in a box. It is about reading tendencies fast so you can communicate in a way the customer actually hears.

**Why it matters on a service call.** You are not just fixing equipment. The customer is making an emotional decision about their home, their safety, and their money. The same explanation lands completely differently depending on how you deliver it. Read the customer right and you cut down on confusion, hesitation, and pushback. Read them wrong and a great recommendation falls flat.

**The two questions that tell you almost everything.** You do not need to diagnose anyone. In the first minute at the door, ask yourself two things:

1. **Pace** — Are they fast-paced or more deliberate and slow?
2. **Focus** — Are they focused on the task, or on people and connection?

Those two answers place them on the map:

| | **Task-focused** | **People-focused** |
|---|---|---|
| **Fast-paced** | **D** — Dominance | **I** — Influence |
| **Slow-paced** | **C** — Conscientiousness | **S** — Steadiness |

A third quick read helps: are they **expressive** (lots of energy, talk, emotion) or **reserved** (quiet, measured, holds back)? Expressive points toward I and S-leaning warmth; reserved points toward D's bluntness and C's precision.

**Bottom line.** Treat DISC like a toolbox. You do not need every tool at once, just the right one for the person in front of you. The faster you spot the style, the faster you build trust.

## Lesson 1: The D Personality — Dominance

**Mindset:** *"Just tell me what it needs and how fast you can do it."*
D stands for **Dominance**. Fast-paced, results-focused, decisive, competitive. Values efficiency, control, and getting it done.

**Core traits**
- Direct and to the point
- Impatient with small talk
- Makes quick decisions
- Can come across as blunt or demanding

**Read them at the door**
- Strong handshake, intense eye contact, upright posture, quick movements
- Asks "How long will this take?" almost immediately
- Speaks in short, declarative statements
- Focused on the end result, not the process

**They say things like:** "What's the bottom line?" • "Skip it, just tell me if it's worth fixing." • "Can you finish today or not?"

**How to communicate**
- Get to the point fast
- Show competence — they respect skill and confidence
- Lead with results, timelines, and impact
- Skip the small talk; stand your ground politely

**How to sell / make the offer**
- Frame it problem → cause → solution, in that order, quickly
- Present solutions, not just a "fix"
- Use urgency tied to control: "If we do this today, you avoid downtime tomorrow."
- Offer two clear options so they feel in command of the decision
- Confidence wins over detail every time

**Avoid:** over-explaining, long technical history, hesitation, too much small talk.

## Lesson 2: The I Personality — Influence

**Mindset:** *"I want to enjoy this interaction and feel good about my choice."*
I stands for **Influence**. Outgoing, optimistic, people-oriented. Thrives on conversation and connection. Motivated by recognition, approval, and excitement.

**Core traits**
- Talkative and expressive
- Relationship-first, business-second
- Energetic and optimistic
- Can get distracted or jump between topics

**Read them at the door**
- Warm greeting, big smile, often a handshake or a touch on the arm
- Open body language; may stand close
- Engages in small talk before any business
- Asks personal questions about you
- Space may feel lively, colorful, personalized

**They say things like:** "Come on in!" • "Oh I love that." • "That's amazing." • lots of stories.

**How to communicate**
- Lead with connection, then get to the point
- Stay upbeat and positive
- Use stories and simple visuals, not heavy data
- Keep them gently on track by linking back to benefits

**How to sell / make the offer**
- Bring enthusiasm to the recommendation
- Paint the picture: comfort, peace of mind, enjoyment, lifestyle
- Keep it light — don't drown them in numbers
- Reinforce with a quick photo or example
- Make the buying decision feel exciting and rewarding

**Avoid:** heavy technical detail, a flat or rushed tone, making it feel like a transaction.

---

## Lesson 3: The S Personality — Steadiness

**Mindset:** *"I want to feel safe, secure, and supported in this decision."*
S stands for **Steadiness**. Calm, cooperative, dependable, loyal. Thrives on stability and security. Motivated by peace, consistency, and avoiding conflict.

**Core traits**
- Calm and patient
- Avoids confrontation and pressure
- Values reliability over flash
- Slower, more thoughtful decisions

**Read them at the door**
- Calm, polite, welcoming but a little reserved
- May avoid eye contact at first, warms up as trust builds
- More reserved body language than a D or an I
- Asks about long-term reliability, not quick fixes
- Home often feels comfortable, organized, modest

**They say things like:** "Will this keep happening?" • "I just want it reliable." • "I want to make sure it's safe for my family."

**How to communicate**
- Be calm, patient, and reassuring
- Go step by step: problem → cause → solution
- Never rush or pressure them
- Emphasize safety, reliability, and peace of mind

**How to sell / make the offer**
- Lead with peace of mind and long-term reliability
- Show how the solution prevents future problems
- Present steady, logical steps rather than flashy upgrades
- Reassure them you'll be there: "I'll be here if you need me."
- Guide them gently to the best fit instead of pushing

**Avoid:** high-pressure tactics, rushing, drastic "rip it all out" framing, hard urgency.

---

## Lesson 4: The C Personality — Conscientiousness

**Mindset:** *"I want to make the right decision with the right information."*
C stands for **Conscientiousness**. Logical, detail-oriented, systematic. Motivated by accuracy, quality, and data. Thrives on structure and dislikes being wrong.

**Core traits**
- Analytical and precise
- Cautious and thorough
- Slower to trust, but commits strongly once convinced
- Dislikes vague answers

**Read them at the door**
- Polite but reserved, not overly warm
- Asks detailed, technical, or "what if" questions
- Home looks tidy, organized, minimalist
- Wants to see reports, documentation, or double-check details

**They say things like:** "Can you show me the numbers?" • "Why exactly did this happen?" • "Does this meet code?"

**How to communicate**
- Be factual, detailed, and precise
- Back everything with data, tests, or documentation
- Use exact figures — "the pressure is 95 PSI," not "the pressure's high"
- Give them time; don't rush the decision

**How to sell / make the offer**
- Lead with accuracy, safety, and compliance
- Show how the recommendation meets standards or code
- Support it with data, warranties, and guarantees
- Present multiple solutions with clear pros and cons
- Earn confidence through facts, never pressure

**Avoid:** winging it, vague claims, pushiness, rushing them to decide.

# PART 2 — PRACTICE SCENARIOS

**All scenarios are residential electrical service calls.** No HVAC, no plumbing — this is built for SHS residential electricians.

**Format for every scenario:**
- A short doorway/job-site description of the customer.
- **Q1 — Identify:** "What personality type is this?" Single answer (D / I / S / C). Instant feedback.
- **Q2 — Sell:** "What's the best way to make your offer to this customer?" Four options, one correct. The wrong options are deliberately the *right* moves for a different type — picking one means you misread the room. Instant feedback.
- Scoring: each question worth 1 point. 24 points total. Pass at 80% (≥ 20 correct).

---

### Scenario 1 — Panel replacement — (Answer: D)
The door swings open before you finish knocking. Firm handshake, strong eye contact. "Appreciate you coming. How long is this gonna take? The panel keeps tripping — just tell me what it needs so it stops." He keeps checking his watch.

**Q1 — What type?** → **D (Dominance).** Fast pace, task-focused, results-first, watching the clock. Classic D.
*Feedback if wrong:* The clock-watching, the firm handshake, and "just tell me what it needs" are speed-and-results signals. That's a D, not a relationship or detail read.

**Q2 — Best way to make the offer?**
- A) ✅ Give the bottom line first — the panel's overloaded, here's why it's tripping, here's the fix and a clear timeline — then offer two options so he stays in control. *Correct: D's buy on confidence and control. Lead with results and let him choose.*
- B) Slow down, reassure him it'll be reliable for years, and tell him you'll always be a call away. *That's the S approach — too slow and soft for a D.*
- C) Walk him through the full load calculation and the panel spec sheet before recommending anything. *That's the C approach — too much detail; he'll feel you're wasting his time.*
- D) Get excited about the upgrade and tell him a story about another happy customer. *That's the I approach — fluff to a D.*

### Scenario 2 — Recurring breaker trips — (Answer: D)
She meets you at the door, all business. "What's the bottom line to stop this breaker from tripping? Skip the backstory — is it worth fixing or do I just replace the panel?" She cuts you off when you start explaining the circuit history.

**Q1 — What type?** → **D (Dominance).** Direct, impatient with detail, wants the bottom line. D.
*Feedback if wrong:* "Skip the backstory" and cutting you off are impatience-with-process signals — a D wants results, fast.

**Q2 — Best way to make the offer?**
- A) ✅ "Here's the problem, here's why the breaker keeps tripping, and here are two ways to fix it — one fast, one permanent." Keep it tight and confident. *Correct: problem → cause → solution, options for control, no fluff.*
- B) Share the exact amperage readings and the panel's full circuit map so she can study it. *C approach — she just told you to skip detail.*
- C) Connect over the house, keep it light, and tell her it'll be worry-free. *I approach — she's not here to chat.*
- D) Reassure her slowly that this is a safe, gentle, long-term fix and there's no rush. *S approach — too slow for a D.*

### Scenario 3 — EV charger install — (Answer: D)
"If the panel can handle it, do it. If not, tell me what it takes. Can you get the charger in this week or not?" He barely looks up from his phone, wants the job scoped and you gone.

**Q1 — What type?** → **D (Dominance).** Decisive, deadline-driven, results-only. D.
*Feedback if wrong:* "Can you get it in this week or not?" is a deadline-and-control signal. That's D.

**Q2 — Best way to make the offer?**
- A) ✅ State whether the panel supports the charger and the impact in one breath, give a firm timeline, and present a do-it-now vs. upgrade-and-do-it-right choice. *Correct: concise, confident, control through options.*
- B) Pull up the load calculation and the NEC charger requirements and review them line by line. *C approach — more detail than a D wants.*
- C) Keep it friendly and upbeat, joke about gas prices, and paint the picture of a worry-free garage. *I approach — he wants speed, not banter.*
- D) Slow down and gently reassure him this is a safe long-term investment for the family. *S approach — too soft and slow.*

### Scenario 7 — Aging panel, safety concern — (Answer: S)
Calm and polite, a little reserved. Warms up as you talk. "I just want to make sure this won't become a problem. We've had enough surprises in this house — I want something I don't have to worry about." Comfortable, tidy, modest home.

**Q1 — What type?** → **S (Steadiness).** Calm, reserved, reliability-focused, conflict-averse. S.

**Q2 — Best way to make the offer?**

- B) Give the blunt bottom line, push urgency, and make her decide today. *D approach — pressure makes an S retreat.*
- C) Bring big energy and a fun story about another customer. *I approach — too much; she wants steadiness.*
- D) Open with the full load calculation and breaker-by-breaker specs. *C approach — more detail than she needs to feel safe.*

### Scenario 10 — Diagnostic / flickering lights — (Answer: C)
Reserved, precise. "Before you recommend anything, can you show me the actual readings? I'd like to see what the voltage is doing at the panel." Tidy, minimalist home. Asks careful "what if" questions.

**Q1 — What type?** → **C (Conscientiousness).** Wants data, precise, reserved, methodical. C.

**Q2 — Best way to make the offer?**

- B) Skip the numbers and give a confident bottom-line recommendation. *D approach — a C wants the evidence, not just your word.*
- C) Bring energy and a feel-good story about a brighter home. *I approach — too light; he wants facts.*
- D) Reassure him gently and focus on peace of mind without the data. *S approach — warmth is fine, but he's asking for proof.*

"I read this house has aluminum wiring and I need to understand exactly what the risk is before I spend money on it." Methodical, a little skeptical. Wants the cause documented and the cost justified.

**Q1 — What type?** → **C (Conscientiousness).** Wants the precise risk, skeptical, needs justification. C.

**Q2 — Best way to make the offer?**

- B) Tell her not to worry and that you'll take good care of her. *S approach — reassurance without the proof she's asking for.*

### Scenario 12 — Code compliance / breaker — (Answer: C)
Polite but cautious. "Does this breaker meet code? Is there documentation? I'd want the manufacturer warranty in writing." Reads everything carefully before deciding.

## Module summary screen (suggested copy)

**You just practiced reading the room.** Every customer is sending signals in the first minute. Pace tells you fast or slow. Focus tells you task or people. - **D** — bottom line, options, control.
- **I** — energy, benefits, keep it light.
- **S** — reassurance, reliability, no pressure.
- **C** — data, proof, documentation.
